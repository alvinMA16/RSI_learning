Abstract
For a finite alphabetAand a rectangleD= [n]×[m], a set of patternsP⊆A D
defines the two-dimensional subshift of finite typeV(P) of all colourings ofZ 2 every
n×mwindow of which lies inP. Kari and Moutot proved that|P| ≤nmforces
a periodic configuration wheneverV(P)̸=∅, and asked for the leastexcessc≥0
such that somePwith|P| ≤nm+chasV(P) aperiodic. No constant upper bound
was known: the classical simulations of Wang tiles by pattern sets pay a per-row and
per-column cost linear in the window dimension.
We prove that the constant is finite and small:
1≤c≤69.
The witness is a set of 73 patterns on a 2×2 window. It is built from a seventeen-tile
Wang tile set of Kari’s multiplier type, reconstructed from scratch: rows carry balanced
(Beatty) representations of reals in [1,3] and multiply them by 3/2 or 1/2; aperiodic-
ity is a four-line telescoping argument ending in 3 a = 2 a+b, and non-emptiness is an
explicit Beatty configuration – no computer search enters either proof. A determinism
lemma then recodes the tiling space, without loss, as a nearest-neighbour (2×2-window)
subshift on the cell alphabet of (multiplier, left-carry) pairs, whose realized window lan-
guage has exactly 73 elements. The non-emptiness, the count 73, and the aperiodicity
are formalized as one theorem in Lean 4 with mathlib, with nosorryand no added
axioms. Within this construction the bound is rigid: deleting any one of the 73 patterns
empties the subshift, soc≤68 is unreachable by shrinking the witness. Complementing
the upper bound, exhaustive and independently cross-checked enumerations show that
a binary alphabet on a 2×2 window admitsnoaperiodicV(P) at any excess, and none
on a 3×2 window withc≤3; the smallest finite case that could decidec= 1 – binary
5×2 with|P|= 11 – is precisely identified and remains open.

Method
The witness is not found by search. It is a Kari-typemultipliertile set [3] – rebuilt from its
mechanism rather than imported – whose aperiodicity proof is algebraic: rows of a tiling
carry balanced representations of reals, vertical adjacency multiplies the represented value
by 3/2 or 1/2, and a doubly periodic tiling would force (3/2) a(1/2)b = 1 witha+b≥1,
i.e. 3 a = 2a+b, which is impossible. The contribution of this paper on top of the classical

7 Open problems
1. Pin the constant: every value in{1, . . . ,69}is currently possible. The construction
class of this paper is exhausted (Remark 5.2); a smaller witness needs either a different
aperiodic mechanism scored by the window language, or the|C|-enumeration frontier
at small alphabets.
2. Decide the binary 5×2,|P|= 11 case (Remark 6.3).
3. Raise the lower bound beyond 1: the annihilator method dies at complexitynm+
1, and the real frontier identified in the companion campaign is the balancedness
hypothesis of [4, Lem. 21] – a combinatorial, not algebraic, obstruction.
4. The general transfer principle behind Lemma 2.3 givesc≤ |L 2×2(X)| −4 forany
aperiodic Wang shiftXpresentable on a 2×2 window, whereL 2×2 is the realized
block language. Score the known small aperiodic tile sets – Jeandel–Rao’s 11 tiles [2],
Culik’s 13 [1] – under this functional.
